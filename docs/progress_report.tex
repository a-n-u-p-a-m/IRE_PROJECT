\section*{Summary}

The project asks which stage of a two-stage retrieval cascade is responsible
for the quality of its output, and answers it by constructing explicit
counterfactual worlds over a frozen document universe. Weeks~1 and~2 are
complete. All infrastructure, all data artefacts and all pre-registered
decisions are in place; no real Stage-2 scores have been computed yet, which is
Week~3.

Concretely: the evaluation universe is frozen at configuration fingerprint
\fp{46d0807d8ed3} with $872{,}455$ query--document pairs; the BM25 Stage-1 run
reproduces published effectiveness exactly; the entire Stage-2 bill is one
measured 27-minute GPU job; $415$ tests pass; and every methodological decision
that could bias a result is recorded in the design log with its reasoning,
before the result exists.

Five defects were found and fixed in shared code during this period, four of
them in checks that were silently vacuous --- they passed while being unable to
detect the failure they existed to catch. Those are described in
Section~\ref{sec:corrections}, because they were the most valuable output of
the fortnight.

\section{The problem and the design}

A cascade retrieves candidates $\Cq = \mathrm{Top}_{\Kc}(\Uq, s_1)$ with a
cheap Stage-1 scorer and reorders them with an expensive Stage-2 reranker
$s_2$. Asking ``which stage did the work'' is not a question about score
correlations; it is a question about what would have happened otherwise.

\subsection{The degeneracy result, and why it motivates the design}

For a \emph{pointwise} Stage-2 scorer, once $\Cq$ is fixed the final ranking is
invariant to $s_1$. Stage~1's only causal channel into the output is
\emph{membership} in $\Cq$ --- not the scores it assigned. This is why
score-level attribution cannot work here, and it is the result the whole
experimental design is built around.

The complement matters as much: \emph{under a budget constraint}, Stage-1 rank
decides which candidates survive the cut, so it becomes causally active again.
That is what the budget worlds measure.

\subsection{Four worlds over a frozen universe}

$\Uq$ is the union of both Stage-1 systems' top-$M$ retrievals, frozen once.
Every claim is relative to it, so regenerating it would invalidate every
previously computed effect; this is enforced mechanically by a fingerprint
sidecar rather than by convention.

\subsection{Three estimands, which do not add up}

\begin{align*}
E_{\text{rerank}} &= U_d(\pi_{\text{act}}) - U_d(\pi_{\text{no2}}) \\
E_{\text{gate}}   &= U_d(\pi_{\text{act}}) - U_d(\pi_{\text{no1}}) \\
\Delta_t          &= U_d(\pi_t) - U_d(\pi_{t=1})
\end{align*}

They are deliberately non-additive. $U_d$ is the per-document contribution to
DCG@$k$ under an explicit gain map.

\subsection{Conditional invariants: the central design point}

The effects obey sign rules that hold only \emph{conditionally on membership}:
$e_{\text{rerank}}$ is exactly zero off $\Cq$; $e_{\text{gate}}$ is
$\geq 0$ for survivors and $\leq 0$ for non-survivors; and $\Delta_t$ has four
membership strata with three of the four signs fixed (removed $\leq 0$,
selected outsider $\geq 0$, unselected outsider $= 0$, retained either sign).

A single unconditional validator would therefore reject correct $\Delta_t$ rows
while passing incorrect $E_{\text{gate}}$ rows. Validation is stratified
throughout, and \texttt{metrics.evaluate\_within\_strata} deliberately returns
no pooled row: because $\mathrm{sign}(E_{\text{gate}})$ is fixed by survivorship
and three of four $\Delta_t$ signs by allocation membership, a pooled statistic
would reward a model for detecting the partition rather than the effect.

\subsection{The gain map}

TREC-DL grade~1 means ``Related'' --- on topic, does not answer the query. The
frozen map is $\{0{:}0,\ 1{:}0,\ 2{:}3,\ 3{:}7\}$. Because every estimand is a
\emph{difference} of utilities, scoring grade~1 as relevant would not cancel;
it would inflate every effect in which a merely related passage moves. Unmapped
grades raise rather than defaulting to zero.

\textbf{Leverage: confirmed.} Candidate Jaccard of $0.11$--$0.16$. Two Stage-1
systems selecting nearly the same documents would leave the cross-paradigm

\textbf{Cross-paradigm confound: retired.} We expected the TREC 2019/2020

DPR's. Measured, the ratio is $1.06\times$ at $\Kc{=}100$ and $1.15\times$ at

both axes that could have spoiled it.

\textbf{Gate-failure supply: thin, and it drove a rule change.} See
Section~\ref{sec:corrections}.

\textbf{Judgment placement: the one open validity threat.} $\Uq$ is $1.65\%$
judged while BM25's top-100 is $51\%$ judged. World~C sorts ${\sim}9{,}000$
documents of which almost none are judged, so judged survivors are displaced
out of the top-10 there, $U_d(\pi_{\text{no1}}) \to 0$, and
$E_{\text{gate}} \to \mathrm{DCG}(\pi_{\text{act}})$ --- the gate would appear
to account for \emph{all} retrieved utility as an artefact of where NIST placed
judgments in 2019. Resolved procedurally at freeze~v2; see
Section~\ref{sec:freeze}.

\subsection{Stage-1 over the universe, and the allocations}

The outsider policies need Stage-1 beyond depth $M$: \texttt{boundary} reads
$s_1$ below the cutoff and \texttt{alternative} reads the other retriever's
ranks over $\Dq = \Uq \setminus \Cq$. Each run was extended to all of $\Uq$
under the floor policy (an unretrieved document scores strictly below
everything the retriever returned --- exactly what it asserted, \emph{not in my
top-M}).

Policy supply turned out to be $40$--$49\times$ the budget at every depth
(boundary $4{,}900/4{,}500/4{,}000$ genuinely ranked documents below the cutoff
against budgets of $100/500/1000$), so both policies are real rather than
nominal: neither has to draw from the tied floored block where the selection
would be the \texttt{doc\_id} tie-break wearing a policy's name.

$11{,}640$ allocations were then published and audited --- $1{,}319{,}200$
promoted documents --- with four invariants all holding: every allocation
spends exactly $B$; the four membership strata partition $\Uq$; $t{=}1$
reproduces the plain cascade, so every $\Delta_t$ is measured against something
that actually ran; and the random control's selection changed under a reseed in
$100\%$ of settings, which is what makes it a control.

\subsection{Reranker and power (contributed)}

Chaitanya measured $535$ pairs/sec on a Colab T4 (MiniLM-L-12-v2,
\texttt{float32}), giving ${\sim}27$ minutes for the whole universe or
${\sim}9$ minutes each under a three-way shard plan. Mean sequence length
${\sim}71$ tokens, max ${\sim}183$, far inside $\texttt{max\_length}{=}512$.
The \textbf{shuffle-invariance test passed on the real reranker}, which
verifies rather than assumes the pointwise property the entire
$E_{\text{gate}}$ derivation rests on.

Shubham's power simulation, re-run at the headline $\Kc{=}100$ and the real
median $|\Uq|$ of $9{,}097$, returns: no subset smaller than all 97 queries
reaches $0.80$ power, and even at 97 only the \texttt{alternative} outsider
policy clears it ($0.935$ continuous, $0.875$ group). \texttt{boundary} falls
short ($0.67$, $0.41$). Therefore \textbf{H7 is confirmatory for
\texttt{alternative} and descriptive for \texttt{boundary}}, with
\texttt{random} as the control, declared before any real effect exists.

\section{Corrections and methodological findings}
\label{sec:corrections}

The most valuable output of the fortnight was five defects in shared code, four
of them in checks that passed while being structurally unable to detect the
failure they existed to catch.

\paragraph{1. The H7 power denominator was conditional.}
\texttt{required\_reference\_size} divided detections by the number of
\emph{testable} draws. A subset with fewer than two queries in a group cannot
be tested at all, which is an H7 failure rather than a draw to discard, so the
reported power answered ``given the subset was testable'' and overstated what a
real subset delivers. Now unconditional, with the conditional figure available
separately. The stale column was \emph{removed} rather than kept, so a
downstream rescaling that would have cancelled the fix raised an error instead
and was found in the same change.

\paragraph{2. ``Relevant'' disagreed with the gain map, by 4--9$\times$.}
\texttt{shared/qrels.py} counted \texttt{relevance > 0}, which includes
grade~1, while the frozen map scores grade~1 as $0.0$. A grade-1 non-survivor
was therefore counted as a gate failure although excluding it costs no utility.
Measured: at $\Kc{=}1000$ the DPR candidate set shows $9$ excluded relevant
documents under the loose definition and exactly $1$ that carries gain. The
headline $\Kc$ was about to be chosen from the first number. Both functions now
take the gain map as a \emph{required} argument, so the definition travels with
the configuration that set it.

\paragraph{3. \texttt{trec\_eval} uses linear gain, not exponential.}
The BM25 verifier must match the definition that produced the published
figures. Implemented from memory as $2^{\text{rel}}-1$, it disagreed with
\texttt{pytrec\_eval} immediately ($0.0799$ against $0.1116$ on the first
random case). \texttt{ndcg\_cut} uses $\text{gain} = \text{rel}$. The wrong
form would have ``verified'' the run against a number it structurally cannot
reproduce, sending us hunting a nonexistent index problem. Pinned by tests that
cross-check against the real \texttt{trec\_eval} C code where installed.

\paragraph{4. The headline-$\Kc$ rule was mis-specified (self-correction).}
The pre-declared rule --- largest $\Kc$ with median gain-carrying
non-survivors $\geq 1$ --- selected $\Kc{=}1000$, where the median supply is
$1$--$2$ and $36$--$39$ of $97$ queries have none at all. The error was
conflating two jobs: ``$\geq 1$'' is a \emph{non-degeneracy floor}, and used as
a \emph{selection} rule it chooses the deepest depth, which is exactly
backwards --- a TREC pool is built from the top ${\sim}100$ of the systems of
the day, so deeper $\Kc$ swallows more of the judged pool into $\Cq$ and leaves
\emph{less} supply outside it.

Separated, the rule takes the depth with greatest supply among those clearing
the floor, which selects $\Kc{=}100$ (median supply $6$). This is a
\emph{post-hoc change to a pre-declared rule} and is recorded as one. It is
defensible only because gate-failure supply is measurable \emph{before} any
effect is computed, so the choice cannot be driven by effect sizes; no
threshold was tuned, and only the direction of the preference changed. A test
feeds the rule a synthetic collection whose supply grows with depth, where it
correctly selects a deeper headline --- the check that it is a rule and not a
constant in disguise.

\paragraph{5. The allocation spend check was vacuous.}
\texttt{validate\_allocation} compared the submitted count against
$\min(n_{\text{out}}, |\text{selected}|)$ --- the amount \emph{achieved} --- so
a short allocation always matched its own shortfall and passed. It accepted an
allocation submitting $1$ of a budget of $2$ with an outsider still available
in $\Dq$: precisely the ``one point of the $t$ curve cheaper than the others''
case its own docstring says it exists to refuse. The ceiling is the available
pool, not the draw. Since \texttt{worlds.py} runs this on every world-D
allocation, the stricter check now applies there too; nothing was relying on
the loose behaviour, verified against all $11{,}640$ real allocations.

\paragraph{6. Feature leakage in the predictors (open).}
Found by Shubham in review, confirmed and sharpened: the Stage-2 features are
built for all top-5{,}000 documents rather than for $\Cq$, while the code
states ``Extra Stage-2 calls $=0$''. For a document outside $\Cq$ a cached
Stage-2 score exists \emph{only because world C was computed}. The consequence
is stronger than mis-stated cost: for a non-survivor $d$,
$\mathrm{rank}_{\pi_{\text{act}}}(d)$ is undefined, so
$e_{\text{gate}}(d) = -U_d(\pi_{\text{no1}})$, and $U_d(\pi_{\text{no1}})$ is
fixed by $\mathrm{rank}_C(d)$, which the $s_2$ scores over $\Uq$ determine
exactly. The feature does not approximate the target; it contains it. Note the
25 tests pass either way --- the suite never asserts the $\Cq$ boundary for
Stage-2 features, which is why it survived review-by-test. Stage-1 features
over $\Uq$ remain legitimately free, since $s_1$ costs no GPU.

\section{Freeze history}
\label{sec:freeze}

\paragraph{Freeze v1 (26 Oct), \fp{46d0807d8ed3}.} Dense Stage~1 is DPR
(\texttt{cosdpr-distil}, replacing ANCE); Stage-1 systems and universe sources
are BM25 and DPR; Stage~2 is a pointwise MiniLM cross-encoder; data is TREC-DL
2019+2020; the gain map gives grades 0 and 1 zero gain.

\paragraph{Freeze v2 (29 Oct), \fp{46d0807d8ed3} --- unchanged.} Both live
decisions looked like configuration changes and neither was one, so the frozen
universe, its sidecar and every manifest written against v1 remain valid.

\textbf{Precision stays \texttt{float32}.} \texttt{float16} would take the fill
from ${\sim}27$ to ${\sim}13$ minutes: a saving of $14$ minutes, once, for the
entire project. Against it, every estimand is a difference of \emph{rank-based}
utilities, and \texttt{float16} perturbs scores at ${\sim}10^{-3}$, which flips
near-ties in a $9{,}000$-document ranking. How many, and whether they land
where they matter, is unquantified --- and quantifying it would cost more GPU
time than the saving. The cost side was already measured, so the decision
needed no further evidence.

\textbf{Unjudged competitors: compute every effect both ways, always.}
Condensed-list evaluation (Sakai, \emph{Alternatives to Bpref}, SIGIR 2007)
drops unjudged documents before ranks are assigned, so a judged document's rank
counts only judged documents above it. Three reasons for computing both:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{It costs no GPU.} Condensing transforms \emph{rankings}, not
scores --- same cache, same $872{,}455$ calls, CPU only.
\item \textbf{It is the only option that does not decide in the dark.} The
measurement that would settle which mode is right --- the judged fraction of
world C's top-10 --- does not exist until the cache is filled. Every other
option commits now to a choice whose evidence arrives later. Pre-registration
exists to fix the \emph{procedure} in advance, not to guess the answer.
\item \textbf{It needs no configuration field.} If both are always computed
there is no choice to encode, so the fingerprint does not move.
\end{itemize}

The \emph{primary} estimand remains the uncondensed one, because it is what a
deployed policy would really do; the condensed figure is reported beside it as
a robustness check. If the two disagree in sign or in which stratum dominates,
that disagreement \emph{is} the finding and gets its own treatment rather than
a footnote. Declared before any effect exists.

\textbf{Also resolved:} cache seeding from existing scores dropped (${\sim}3$
minutes of a 27-minute bill, against interleaving two scoring runs); SPLADE out
(a third Stage-1 system would require rebuilding the frozen universe, and the
Jaccard already gives ample leverage); hashing out per written criteria;
reference subset all 97 queries. No open decisions remain in the log.

\section{Future work}

\subsection{Week 3 --- fill the cache, compute real effects}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Blocking:} the PR \#3 leakage fix, after which the synthetic
fidelity numbers must be regenerated and all three baselines will move.
\item Fill the Stage-2 cache over $\Uq$ under the three-way shard plan:
${\sim}9$ minutes each, resumable and shardable with a merge step.
\item Compute $E_{\text{rerank}}$, $E_{\text{gate}}$ and $\Delta_t$ over the
real universe, in both the uncondensed and condensed modes, with stratified
validation throughout.
\item \textbf{The decisive measurement:} the judged fraction of world C's
top-10. If it is ${\sim}20\%$ unjudged, the judgment-placement concern is a
footnote; if ${\sim}95\%$, $E_{\text{gate}}$ as primarily specified is not
measuring the gate and the condensed figure becomes the headline.
\item Diagnosis labels and the H7 test, with group sizes reported beside every
$p$-value, confirmatory only for \texttt{alternative}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Week 4 --- analysis and reporting}

Effect distributions by stratum; the cost--fidelity curve, which must converge
to the exact reference at full budget; utility versus $t$; the H7 comparison;
a worked gating-failure example. Predictor fidelity against all three baselines
(zero, membership-only, stratum-mean), fitted on training queries only. Full
compute accounting. The reproducibility pack --- already largely demonstrated
by three bit-identical universe rebuilds across two operating systems.

\subsection{Known risks}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Judgment sparsity} is the main threat to $E_{\text{gate}}$ and is
mitigated procedurally rather than solved. The decisive measurement is
scheduled for the moment scores exist.
\item \textbf{H7 is descriptive for \texttt{boundary}} at any feasible subset
size. This is a declared limitation, not a result to be improved by tuning.
\item \textbf{Gate-failure supply is thin even at the headline depth} --- a
median of 6 gain-carrying non-survivors, with 17--23 of 97 queries having none.
Per-query effect distributions will be noisy and must be reported with their
supply.
\item \textbf{Grade-1 prevalence} meant the usable relevant supply was
4--9$\times$ smaller than the naive count. Any future collection needs the same
check before its $\Kc$ is chosen.
\end{itemize}
